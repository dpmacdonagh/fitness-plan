Every movement in the plan, with an animated demo of the correct form. The
figures loop like a GIF — watch the shape the body makes, then match it.
Written cues matter more than speed: **slow and controlled beats fast, every
time.**

The three core moves (curl-up, side plank, bird dog) are the **McGill Big 3**
— chosen specifically because they build core stiffness *without* bending or
compressing the spine. That's the whole point given the disc history: brace,
don't bend.

<h2 id="curl-up">Curl-up (McGill)</h2>

<figure class="demo">
  <svg data-rig="curl-up" role="img" aria-label="Animated demo: McGill curl-up — lying on the back, one knee bent, hands under the low back, head and shoulders rise a few centimeters as one unit, hold, lower"></svg>
  <figcaption>The lift is tiny — shoulder blades barely clear the floor. The spine never bends.</figcaption>
</figure>

- Lie on your back. One knee bent, foot flat; the other leg straight.
- Slide both hands under the arch of your low back — they're there to
  **preserve the arch**, not cushion it. Don't flatten your back into the floor.
- Lift head and shoulders **as one unit**, just a few centimeters — imagine
  your head resting on a bathroom scale and you're taking the weight off it.
- No chin poke, no crunching. If your low back moves, you went too high.
- Hold about 8 seconds, breathing normally. Lower slowly. Swap legs halfway
  through your sets.
- **Week 1 dose:** 5 per side.

**Why this one:** it trains the abs isometrically with zero spinal flexion —
a crunch without the crunch. Loaded spinal flexion is permanently off the
menu (rule 1); this is the substitute, straight from the spine-rehab
literature.

<h2 id="side-plank">Side plank, from knees</h2>

<figure class="demo">
  <svg data-rig="side-plank" role="img" aria-label="Animated demo: side plank from knees — lying on one side propped on the forearm, knees bent, hips lift until shoulder, hip and knee form a straight line, hold, lower"></svg>
  <figcaption>Elbow directly under the shoulder. Hips drive up until the body is one straight line to the knees.</figcaption>
</figure>

- Lie on your side, propped on your forearm. **Elbow directly under the
  shoulder** — this stacks the joint so the arm just holds position, it
  doesn't press. That matters for the left-arm rule.
- Knees bent to about 90°, one on top of the other.
- Drive your hips up until shoulder–hip–knee make a straight line. Don't let
  the hips sag back down or roll forward.
- Hold, breathing normally, then lower with control. Do both sides.
- **Left side caution:** you're supporting on the left forearm when the left
  side is down. The elbow-under-shoulder position keeps the arm short and
  stacked — that's allowed. If the pec-to-elbow line lights up at all, the
  set ends immediately (rule 2).
- **Week 1 dose:** 10-second holds per side.

<h2 id="bird-dog">Bird dog</h2>

<figure class="demo">
  <svg data-rig="bird-dog" role="img" aria-label="Animated demo: bird dog — on hands and knees, the opposite arm and leg extend out to horizontal while the back stays flat, hold, return"></svg>
  <figcaption>Opposite arm and leg reach out level with the floor. The back stays table-flat — nothing tips or twists.</figcaption>
</figure>

- Hands and knees. Hands under shoulders, knees under hips, back flat like a
  table.
- Reach one arm forward and the **opposite** leg back, both to horizontal.
  The goal is a straight line from fingertips to heel.
- The hard part is what *doesn't* move: hips stay level, back stays flat, no
  arching or twisting. Imagine balancing a cup of coffee on your low back.
- Hold a few seconds, return with control, switch sides.
- **Left arm note:** reaching forward with a straight arm is fine here — the
  arm is unloaded (nothing in the hand, no pressing). If it ever complains,
  point the thumb up and reach a little less far, or just extend the leg only.
- **Week 1 dose:** 5 per side.

<h2 id="glute-bridge">Glute bridge</h2>

<figure class="demo">
  <svg data-rig="glute-bridge" role="img" aria-label="Animated demo: glute bridge — lying on the back with knees bent and feet flat, hips lift until shoulders, hips and knees form a straight line, hold, lower"></svg>
  <figcaption>Squeeze the glutes to lift — the finish is a straight line from shoulders to knees. Don't arch past it.</figcaption>
</figure>

- Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat about hip-width, arms relaxed at
  your sides.
- Squeeze your glutes and push through your heels to lift the hips until
  shoulders–hips–knees form a straight line.
- **Stop at straight.** Lifting higher just cranks the low back — the glutes
  are done working at the top.
- Pause a second at the top, lower with control.
- This is *the* staple for the hips and hamstrings (rule 4) — it strengthens
  them with zero spinal load. Stop each set 3–4 reps short of grinding.
- **Week 1 dose:** 2×10.

<h2 id="incline-pushup">Incline push-up</h2>

<figure class="demo">
  <svg data-rig="incline-pushup" role="img" aria-label="Animated demo: incline push-up — hands on a counter-height surface, body in a straight line from head to heels, chest lowers to the surface and presses back up"></svg>
  <figcaption>Body rigid as a plank from head to heels. Chest to the counter, press away — elbows about 45° from the body.</figcaption>
</figure>

- Hands on a sturdy counter, table, or wall-bar at about chest height,
  slightly wider than shoulders.
- Walk your feet back until your body is a straight line from head to heels.
  Brace like a plank — no sagging hips.
- Lower your chest to the surface, elbows tracking about 45° from your body
  (not flared straight out), then press back to straight arms.
- **Left arm rules apply hard here** (rule 2): keep the press close to the
  body, never lock out hard into a fully extended reach, and the *instant*
  the pec-to-elbow line lights up, the set is over. The steeper the incline,
  the lighter the load — steeper is always the right call in doubt.
- **Week 1 dose:** 2×8.

<h2 id="chair-squat">Chair-assisted squat</h2>

<figure class="demo">
  <svg data-rig="chair-squat" role="img" aria-label="Animated demo: chair-assisted squat — standing in front of a chair, hips push back and down until they tap the seat, arms reach forward for balance, then stand back up"></svg>
  <figcaption>Hips go back first, like sitting down. Tap the chair — don't plop — and stand back up through the heels.</figcaption>
</figure>

- Stand in front of a sturdy chair, feet about shoulder-width.
- Push your **hips back first** — like reaching your butt for the seat — then
  bend the knees. Arms reach forward as a counterweight.
- Lower with control until you lightly tap the chair. **Tap, don't sit.** If a
  rep is failing, sitting down fully is the built-in safety net — that's why
  the chair is there.
- Stand back up by driving through your heels and squeezing your glutes.
- Keep the chest up; the torso leans forward naturally but the back stays
  neutral, never rounded (rule 1 — no loaded spinal flexion).
- **Week 1 dose:** 2×8.

## The cardio (no demo needed)

- **Walking:** brisk enough that you're breathing deeper but can still talk in
  full sentences. Compression shorts + anti-chafe balm every time (rule 3).
- **Elliptical:** conversational pace. If you can't speak a sentence, slow
  down — the aerobic base that eventually bikes across America is built at
  *easy* pace, not by suffering. Same skin protocol as walking.
